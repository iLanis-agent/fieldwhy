FieldWhy

Why did awk give you that many fields?

Enter a line and an FS. FieldWhy shows how awk reads that FS (blank runs, one literal character, or a regex), the fields it produces, and what happens to $0 when you assign to a field or to NF.

Open the app

Traps it explains: FS=" " versus FS="[ ]", FS="|" being literal while FS="||" is a regex, a leading empty field with FS=" +", and $1=$1 collapsing your spacing.

How it was tested

Against GNU awk 5.1.0 on 198,000 random lines (6 seeds), each run through four programs (split, $1=$1, $(NF+2)="X", NF=2) with 33 FS values: blank, 10 single characters, empty, and 21 regexes. 792,000 comparisons, 0 mismatches. mawk 1.3.4 agrees on all 413,148 ASCII comparisons; on lines with non-ASCII characters it works on bytes, so those differ and are excluded. FS values were passed through ENVIRON so no escape processing got in the way. A first seed was used while building (it found $1=$1 on an empty line, which gives NF=1).

Known gap: FS regexes with alternation (a|ab). awk takes the longest match, this page takes the first alternative: 3,845 of 120,000 such comparisons differed, and the app warns when it sees one. Not covered: RS, FPAT, FIELDWIDTHS, -Ft, IGNORECASE, FS regexes that can match nothing. Entirely client-side.
